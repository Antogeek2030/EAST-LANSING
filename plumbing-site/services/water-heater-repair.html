Water Heater Issues We Fix in East Lansing

1. No Hot Water

The cause depends on heater type. On electric models, a tripped circuit breaker or high-limit reset, a failed heating element, or a faulty thermostat are common. On gas models, the pilot light may be out, the thermocouple may have failed, or the gas control valve may need service. We inspect the unit and address the problem.

2. Water That Is Neither Hot Nor Cold

If water does not heat properly or only stays hot briefly, one heating element may have failed. Sediment can insulate the water from the heat source. A broken dip tube can send cold water to the top of the tank instead of the bottom. We diagnose and fix these issues without unnecessary replacement.

3. Banging, Popping, and Rumbling Noises

Those sounds are usually sediment. Minerals settle at the bottom of the tank, harden, and create pockets that boil and pop. Hard water accelerates the buildup. A professional flush can clear the tank, restore normal operation, and reduce stress on elements or the burner. Heavy sediment shortens heater life and can cause overheating if ignored.

4. Leaks

Leaks at a drain valve, inlet/outlet connection, or fitting are often straightforward repairs. A leaking temperature-and-pressure (T&P) relief valve may indicate excess pressure or temperature, or a faulty valve—never cap or plug this safety valve. Water leaking from the bottom or jacket of the tank usually means corrosion; if repair is not possible we will explain replacement options before a small leak becomes a flood. For related pipe and connection leaks, see leak detection and pipe repair.

5. Rusty, Discolored, or Smelly Water

A corroding tank or depleted anode rod can cause rusty hot water. If discoloration appears only on the hot side, the heater is the likely source. A rotten-egg smell in hot water often comes from bacteria reacting with the anode rod; replacing the rod usually fixes it. A gas smell near the heater is a different issue and a safety emergency—call immediately.

6. Pilot Light and Burner Problems

A pilot that will not stay lit or a burner that will not ignite may point to a dirty pilot assembly, worn thermocouple, defective gas valve, or poor combustion air. Gas appliances involve combustion and venting and should be serviced by a licensed professional.

7. Pressure and Valve Problems

A dripping T&P valve, banging pipes, or fluctuating pressure can come from thermal expansion, a faulty valve, or high incoming pressure. We test the valve, check system pressure, and install an expansion tank when needed to keep your plumbing safe.

8. Emergency Water Heater Repair

A burst or severely leaking tank can flood a basement or utility room in minutes. Turn off the water and the power or gas, then call us. Our emergency plumbing team responds quickly.

FAQs

How much does water heater repair cost in East Lansing?

Cost depends on the problem and the part. Replacing a thermocouple, thermostat, heating element, or relief valve is usually a smaller repair. A leaking tank generally cannot be repaired and needs replacement. We diagnose the issue, explain the price, and give you an estimate before starting work.

Why is my water heater not producing hot water?

On electric models: tripped breaker, high-limit reset, failed element, or bad thermostat. On gas models: pilot out, failed thermocouple, or faulty gas control valve. If a quick check does not solve it, call a plumber.

Why does my hot water run out so fast?

Sediment buildup, a failed lower heating element, or a damaged dip tube are common causes. The heater may also be undersized for your household demand or nearing the end of its life.

Why is my water heater leaking?

Leaks from a drain valve, fitting, or connection are often simple repairs. A leaking T&P valve can signal excess pressure/temperature or a bad valve. A leak from the tank itself usually means the tank has corroded and needs replacement. Shut off the cold supply and cut power or gas until it is inspected.

What should I do if my water heater is leaking?

Shut off the cold water supply valve feeding the heater. Then cut power at the breaker (electric) or turn the gas control to “off” (gas). Move belongings away from the water and take photos for insurance. If water is near outlets or the electrical panel, stay clear and call a plumber (and electrician if needed).
